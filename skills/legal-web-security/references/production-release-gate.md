# Production release gate: paperwork, consent, and database authorization

Use this reference when a product is approaching a production release and the work combines legal artifacts, analytics or session replay, authentication, authorization, database policies, or a request for a reusable pre-launch prompt.

This is an engineering release gate and issue-spotting aid. It is not legal advice, a legal opinion, a compliance certification, a security certification, or a substitute for qualified counsel and an appropriately scoped security review. A passing gate means only that the listed evidence was checked.

## Claim triage from social-media advice

Do not copy an attractive claim into a policy, code comment, sales page, or launch decision without an authority and facts.

| Transcript claim | Safe treatment |
| --- | --- |
| “A missing policy costs `$2,500` per violation.” | Not a universal rule. A penalty depends on the jurisdiction, statute or regulator, entity, conduct, timing, orders, and facts. Ask for the primary authority and counsel review before stating a number. |
| “Analytics or session replay violations cost `$5K` per violation.” | Not a universal rule. Identify the exact law, territory, claimant or regulator, counting method, and current authority. Do not use a fixed number as a release threshold. |
| “Finish the terms, privacy policy, and cookie notice before writing code.” | Useful sequencing, but the documents must match real collection, sharing, retention, rights, payment, and deletion behavior. Early drafts need labeled assumptions and must be revisited after implementation. |
| “PostHog session replay is off by default.” | Do not assume this. At the time checked, PostHog's official installation documentation described session replay as recording sessions when configured, and its official SDK snippets exposed capture opt-in/opt-out and recording controls. Exact behavior is SDK, version, platform, project, and configuration dependent. Inspect the deployed version and configuration. |
| “A banner means analytics consent is handled.” | A banner is only one UI component. Verify the default state, category-specific choices, SDK initialization, network calls, withdrawal behavior, preference storage, masking, retention, vendor terms, and regional requirements. |
| “RLS protects the database.” | Row-level security can be an important enforcement layer, but it is not a complete authorization model. Check API routes, service-role or admin paths, jobs, exports, storage, caches, search indexes, webhooks, and provider bypasses. |
| “A leak will shut down the business.” | Treat this as risk rhetoric. Model the actual assets, blast radius, regulatory and contractual exposure, recovery objectives, communications plan, and business impact. |

## Gate 1 — paperwork and product facts

Before release, build a facts-to-behavior-to-document crosswalk. Use the repository's [web-compliance checklist](web-compliance-checklist.md) and [legal-template map](legal-template-map.md) to select starting points; do not publish a template unchanged.

### Required fact inventory

Record the answer, owner, source, and status for each item:

- business legal name, operating address or service address, support and privacy contacts, and the countries or states served;
- product surfaces, account types, paid features, subscriptions, trials, renewals, taxes or fees shown at checkout, cancellation, refunds, and chargebacks;
- data collected directly, inferred, generated by an AI feature, or received from a third party;
- cookies, local storage, device identifiers, analytics events, session replay, advertising pixels, crash logs, and support tooling;
- vendors, subprocessors, hosting regions, transfer mechanisms, contracts or DPAs, retention, deletion, backups, and access roles;
- children or age-restricted use, health, biometric, precise location, financial, credential, employment, or other sensitive data;
- user-generated content, moderation, intellectual-property complaints, takedowns, feedback boards, and account deletion;
- commercial email, transactional email, unsubscribe and preference-center behavior;
- accessibility behavior and any product, AI, safety, performance, savings, or security claim shown to users.

### Artifact checklist

Create or update only the artifacts that fit the facts and distribution model:

1. Privacy policy or privacy notice, including categories, purposes, sources, disclosures, transfers, retention, rights, deletion, contact route, and material changes.
2. Terms of service or use, including eligibility, account rules, acceptable use, user content, intellectual-property permissions, suspension, paid terms, disclaimers, liability, and dispute provisions selected with counsel.
3. Cookie policy or notice, plus a preference center where the relevant law or product choice requires one.
4. Refund, cancellation, renewal, and chargeback policy that agrees with checkout and payment-provider behavior.
5. Vendor/subprocessor disclosures and DPAs or other required agreements.
6. Business details, support route, data-deletion request option, age/children safeguards, and commercial-email unsubscribe path where applicable.

For every published statement, link it to the implementation that makes it true. Mark placeholders, unresolved jurisdictions, retention decisions, and counsel questions visibly. A policy link does not repair an implementation mismatch.

### Paperwork gate prompt

Use this standalone prompt with an authorized coding assistant:

```text
Run a read-only production paperwork and product-facts audit for this repository. Do not publish a policy, invent a jurisdiction, add a legal conclusion, change production, send a notice, or commit secrets.

Inspect the actual routes, forms, schemas, cookies, local storage, analytics/SDK initialization, payment flows, email flows, deletion flows, vendor integrations, uploads, user-generated content, and public claims. Build a matrix with:

- artifact or disclosure;
- fact it must state;
- implementation location;
- vendor or contract dependency;
- evidence checked;
- status: verified, not verified, assumed, not applicable with reason, or needs counsel;
- owner and next action.

Check privacy notice, terms, cookie notice/preference center, refund/cancellation language, business details, deletion request path, age/children handling, email unsubscribe, vendor/DPA disclosures, AI/data-use disclosures, accessibility, and claims substantiation. Compare prices and mandatory fees in the UI, checkout, receipts, and policy documents.

Do not copy fixed penalty numbers or social-media advice into the result. For each legal claim, require a current primary source, jurisdiction, facts, and counsel owner. Preserve visible placeholders instead of guessing. Use synthetic data and redacted evidence only. Finish with the smallest set of release blockers and the exact verification needed for each.
```

## Gate 2 — consent-aware analytics and session replay

Analytics and session replay are separate data-flow decisions. Treat session replay as capable of recording interaction details, not as harmless pageview telemetry. PostHog's official documentation says the customer is responsible for deciding what data to collect and how it complies, and describes controls for collection, processing, and storage. Its session-replay documentation also describes masking and recording rules. Re-check the current documentation and deployed SDK before release:

- [PostHog privacy compliance](https://posthog.com/docs/privacy)
- [PostHog session replay installation](https://posthog.com/docs/session-replay/installation)
- [PostHog session replay](https://posthog.com/docs/session-replay)
- [PostHog sessions](https://posthog.com/docs/data/sessions)

### Inventory the actual behavior

For each analytics, replay, error, advertising, experimentation, or support SDK, record:

- package, version, initialization location, project token type, API host, region, and environment;
- events, autocapture, pageviews, identifiers, user properties, URLs, referrers, form fields, console data, network data, and replay screenshots or touch/keyboard behavior;
- cookies, local storage, session identifiers, device identifiers, feature flags, and cross-device or cross-subdomain linking;
- masking, blocking, redaction, sampling, recording rules, retention, deletion, access roles, subprocessors, and DPA/contract status;
- consent category, legal or product basis, pre-consent behavior, opt-in/opt-out calls, withdrawal behavior, and whether the SDK can be initialized without capture;
- whether the SDK is loaded by the app, tag manager, framework plugin, browser extension, or a vendor dashboard setting.

### Safe implementation expectations

- Treat an unknown preference as no permission for optional analytics, advertising, or session replay.
- If consent is the selected basis or a jurisdiction requires it, do not load or initialize optional capture before the relevant choice. If the provider supports a no-capture initialization mode, verify that mode against the current SDK documentation and test the network behavior; do not infer it from a method name.
- Keep necessary service operation separate from optional analytics and marketing categories. Do not force optional tracking as the price of using an unrelated core feature without a documented decision and counsel review.
- Make reject, accept-by-category, and withdraw controls as clear and usable as accept. Store the minimum preference record needed to honor the choice.
- Withdrawal must stop future optional calls. It does not automatically erase historical vendor data; connect the request to the vendor's deletion process and the product's retention policy.
- Mask or block passwords, payment data, authentication tokens, health data, private messages, and other sensitive values before capture. Masking is not a replacement for a required consent decision.
- Never put a personal API key, private vendor token, or secret in browser code. A public project token can still identify a data destination and must be handled consistently with the privacy disclosure.

### Verification matrix

Use a clean browser profile and synthetic account/data in an authorized staging environment. Capture request names and destinations, not tokens or personal content.

| State | Expected evidence |
| --- | --- |
| First visit, no choice | No optional analytics/replay initialization or data request when consent is required; only the minimum preference mechanism runs. |
| Reject analytics/replay | No optional event, replay, cookie, storage identifier, or vendor request after the rejection. |
| Accept analytics only | Analytics behavior matches the selected category; replay and marketing remain off. |
| Accept replay | Replay starts only after the choice; sensitive fields remain masked or blocked. |
| Withdraw | Future optional capture stops, the preference changes, and supported local identifiers are cleared or disabled. |
| Logged-in synthetic user | No password, token, payment value, private message, or unnecessary profile data appears in events, replay, network capture, or logs. |
| Subdomains and reloads | The recorded preference is scoped and honored consistently without silently broadening consent. |
| Vendor/project setting changed | Application behavior remains safe or fails closed; document dashboard settings and re-test after SDK upgrades. |

### Consent and session-replay audit prompt

```text
Run a read-only consent and analytics/session-replay audit on this repository in an authorized staging environment only. Use synthetic accounts and values. Do not access unrelated systems, capture real user data, change production settings, rotate credentials, or send test data to a real vendor project unless the owner explicitly authorized that exact test project.

Find every analytics, session replay, advertising, experimentation, error, support, tag-manager, and AI telemetry integration. Inspect package versions, initialization order, consent state machine, cookies/local storage, network destinations, event properties, identifiers, replay recording rules, masking/blocking, retention/deletion settings, vendor/DPA records, and environment configuration.

Determine whether optional capture occurs before a required or selected consent choice. Test a clean profile through: no choice, reject, accept analytics only, accept replay, withdraw, reload, subdomain navigation, logged-in synthetic user, and sensitive-field entry. Verify request timing and destinations in the Network panel and inspect storage without printing tokens. Verify that withdrawal stops future capture and that historical deletion is handled separately.

Do not assume PostHog or any other vendor is compliant, that a banner is sufficient, that session replay is off by default, or that masking equals consent. Check the current vendor documentation for the exact SDK version and record the URL/date/configuration checked. Report findings with file/config locations, expected versus observed behavior, redacted evidence, severity, and a minimal remediation. Do not claim legal compliance.
```

## Gate 3 — authentication, authorization, and database policies

Use precise terms:

- **Authentication** establishes which account, service, or session is making a request.
- **Authorization** decides whether that principal may perform this action on this object, field, tenant, or workflow in this state.
- **Row-level security (RLS)** is a database-enforcement mechanism that can constrain rows for supported roles and queries. It does not automatically secure every API, privileged connection, background job, cache, export, file, or external system.

### Authorization requirements

Before release, define an access matrix for each protected resource and operation:

| Principal | Read | Create | Update | Delete | Export/share | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unauthenticated |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Owner/member |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Other tenant/member |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Administrator/support |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Background job/service |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Then verify that:

- every protected route performs server-side authorization on the requested object and tenant;
- default behavior is deny, and object IDs, roles, tenant IDs, prices, ownership fields, and entitlements are not trusted from the client;
- client code contains no master database key, service-role credential, private vendor secret, or privileged endpoint;
- RLS policies are enabled for every relevant table and tested for the actual application role, including insert, update, delete, joins, nested queries, and views;
- service-role or admin paths are isolated, narrow, logged, reviewed, and not reachable through ordinary user requests;
- jobs, webhooks, admin/support tools, exports, downloads, search indexes, caches, storage objects, and backups enforce equivalent scope;
- users cannot change ownership, tenant, role, billing state, moderation state, or other protected fields through mass assignment or crafted requests;
- unauthorized reads and writes return a safe denial without revealing whether another user's object exists, and errors do not expose data;
- negative tests cover two synthetic accounts and two synthetic tenants, including direct URL/request replay. See the [authorized object-access replay procedure](vibe-coded-app-security-cheatsheet.md#authorized-manual-replay-test).

### Database authorization audit prompt

```text
Run a read-only authorization and database-policy audit for this repository. Use only an explicitly authorized staging/test deployment, synthetic accounts, and synthetic tenants. Do not probe a third-party or unrelated live service, use real personal data, print cookies/tokens, change production policies, disable controls, or run destructive queries.

Map authentication, session creation, authorization middleware, route handlers, data-access code, database roles, RLS policies, storage rules, background jobs, webhooks, admin/support tools, exports, caches, search, and backups. Build an access matrix for unauthenticated users, owners, other users, other tenants, administrators/support, and service jobs across read/create/update/delete/export/share operations.

For every protected operation, verify server-side object and tenant checks, deny-by-default behavior, protected-field handling, and safe errors. Confirm that no browser bundle or public environment variable contains a master/service-role key. Inspect whether privileged connections bypass RLS and whether equivalent authorization exists in every bypass path.

Run low-volume negative tests with synthetic data: user A must not read, mutate, download, export, or infer user B's or tenant B's objects; a member must not become an administrator by changing request fields; an expired or revoked session must fail; and a job/webhook must be scoped to its intended tenant. Record request shape and status/result only, with identifiers redacted.

Report each gap with the exact file, route, policy, role, or configuration; attack path; severity and confidence; expected denial; observed evidence; minimal remediation; regression test; and remaining unverified paths. Do not call the system secure because RLS is enabled or because authentication succeeds.
```

## Combined pre-production prompt

```text
Run a pre-production legal, consent, and database-authorization gate for this repository. Work read-only first. Use only the repository and an explicitly authorized staging environment with synthetic data. Do not access unrelated systems, collect secrets or personal data, change production, publish legal text, rotate credentials, delete data, or claim compliance/security certification.

1. Build a facts-to-behavior-to-document matrix for privacy notice, terms, cookie notice/preference center, refund/cancellation, business details, deletion requests, age/children handling, email unsubscribe, vendor/DPA disclosures, AI/data-use notices, accessibility, and public claims. Mark verified, not verified, assumed, not applicable with reason, or needs counsel. Reject invented jurisdictions and unsupported penalty numbers.

2. Inventory every analytics, session replay, advertising, error, experimentation, support, tag-manager, and AI telemetry SDK. Check initialization order, consent categories, cookies/storage, network calls, data fields, identifiers, vendor regions, retention, deletion, masking, recording rules, and contract/DPA status. In staging, test no choice, reject, accept by category, withdraw, reload, subdomains, logged-in synthetic users, and sensitive fields. Verify no optional capture occurs before the required/selected choice and that withdrawal stops future capture.

3. Separate authentication from authorization. Map the access matrix, route checks, database roles, RLS policies, storage, jobs, webhooks, admin/support, exports, caches, search, and backups. Run authorized synthetic negative tests for cross-user and cross-tenant reads, writes, downloads, exports, field tampering, role escalation, expired sessions, and service-role paths. Verify that public code contains no master/service credential.

4. Return a release matrix with blocker, source, implementation location, evidence, owner, remediation, regression test, and residual risk. Include exact commands and test states. Keep all evidence redacted. Escalate legal interpretation to counsel and architectural/security risk to the security owner. Do not convert a checklist pass into a statement that the product is legally compliant or secure.
```

## Evidence pack and stop conditions

Keep a small, reproducible release packet:

| Area | Minimum evidence before a release decision |
| --- | --- |
| Paperwork | Approved, fact-matched URLs or files; business/contact owner; version/date; unresolved counsel questions. |
| Data flows | SDK/vendor inventory; event and replay field map; destination/region; retention/deletion owner; DPA/contract status. |
| Consent | State-machine tests and redacted Network/Storage evidence for no choice, reject, accept-by-category, withdrawal, reload, and subdomain cases. |
| Authorization | Access matrix; route/policy mapping; negative tests for other user/tenant, mutation, export, storage, job, and admin paths. |
| Secrets | Repository and build-output scan results; confirmation that privileged keys remain server-side; redacted environment/config review. |
| Recovery | Backup, deletion, incident, vendor-deletion, and rollback owners with tested evidence where the product promises them. |

Stop the release or escalate when any of these is true:

- a policy or contract contains a material placeholder or contradicts observed behavior;
- the applicable jurisdiction, audience, sensitive-data category, or vendor role is unknown and changes the decision;
- optional analytics or replay fires before a required/selected choice, or withdrawal does not stop future capture;
- a user can read or mutate another user's or tenant's data, or a privileged path bypasses the intended checks;
- a master/service key is exposed, logs contain secrets or sensitive values, or evidence cannot be collected without real data;
- a payment, pricing, AI, security, or compliance claim has no owner and substantiation;
- a proposed fix is destructive, production-only, or cannot be verified safely in staging.
